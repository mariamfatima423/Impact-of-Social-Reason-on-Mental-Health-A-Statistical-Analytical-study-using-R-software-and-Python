# Impact of Social Factors on Mental Health: A Statistical Study

A statistical analysis of how social and economic indicators relate to a country's Mental Health Quotient (MHQ), using Python and R.

**Context:** MSc Statistics group project, Department of Statistics and Operations Research, Aligarh Muslim University (2022-23), supervised by Prof. Qazi Mazhar Ali.

## Objectives
1. Examine the effects of various social factors on the Mental Health Quotient (MHQ).
2. Find the correlation between MHQ score and each social factor.
3. Identify which continents show high variation in MHQ and the social factors.
4. Make recommendations for improving MHQ scores.

## Data
Secondary data for **33 countries** from public reports (2021):

| Variable | Source |
|---|---|
| Mental Health Quotient (MHQ) | Sapien Labs, Mental State of the World 2021 |
| Economic participation, education attainment, health and survival, political empowerment | World Economic Forum, Global Gender Gap Report 2021 |
| Multidimensional Poverty Index | UNDP and OPHI, Global MPI 2021 |
| Human Development Index | UNDP, Human Development Report 2021/2022 |
| Gross Domestic Product | World Bank |
| Global Health Security Index, Climate Change Performance Index | GHS Index 2021, CCPI 2021 |

## Methods
- Exploratory data analysis (summary statistics, continent-wise analysis)
- Correlation analysis between MHQ and each indicator
- Simple linear regression and multiple linear regression
- Multicollinearity check (VIF)
- Logistic regression
- Tools: R and Python

## Key Findings
- **Positive correlation with MHQ:** women's economic participation and opportunity, the health and survival gender gap, and the Multidimensional Poverty Index.
- **Negative correlation with MHQ:** women's education attainment, women's political empowerment, GDP, Human Development Index, Global Health Security Index and Climate Change Performance Index.
- Higher national wealth and development did not go with better mental health. For example, the USA and UK scored below average on MHQ despite top scores on health security and climate performance.
- The study concludes that countries need balanced progress across social needs, not economic growth alone.

**Note:** these are correlations across 33 countries, not proof of cause and effect.
